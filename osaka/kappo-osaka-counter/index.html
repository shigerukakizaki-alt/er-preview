Kappo: why the counter is a stage in Osaka

Cut and simmer, in front of you. A short guide to the counter cuisine that Osaka is said to have started.

Kappo is written with two characters: katsu, to cut, and po, to simmer. At a kappo restaurant the chef prepares each dish in front of you, across a counter of polished wood. The counter style is said to have begun in Osaka.

The counter is the stage

Unlike a fixed kaiseki course served in a private room, kappo is a conversation. The chef watches how you eat, and the next dish follows. Sit at the counter and you see every cut of the knife.

Lift the lid, smell first

The clear soup in a lacquered bowl is often called the heart of Japanese cuisine. When you lift the lid, take in the aroma before you taste. It is also why guests are asked not to wear strong perfume.

Who explains it all

On our kappo evening, a food writer who has written about Osaka's restaurants for many years sits with you. Between dishes they explain the season, the knife work, the tableware and the history of the house, and help you talk with the chef.
