Shrine or temple? How to tell them apart in Osaka

They often stand side by side, but they belong to two different faiths. Three things to look for, and how to pray at each.

Walk through any Japanese city and you will pass both. Shrines belong to Shinto, Japan's indigenous faith. Temples are Buddhist, a faith that came from the continent some 1,500 years ago. Most Japanese people visit both, at different moments of life.

Look at the gate

A shrine is marked by a torii, a gate of two posts and two crossbeams, often painted vermilion. A temple usually has a large roofed gate, often with fierce guardian statues on either side.

Look for incense

Smoke rising from a large bronze burner almost always means a temple. Shrines use water and white paper streamers to purify, not incense.

Pray in the right way

At a shrine, bow twice, clap twice and bow once more. At a temple, put your palms together quietly, without clapping. If you are unsure, watch the people around you, or ask us. Everyone is welcome at both.

Osaka is home to some of the oldest shrines in Japan. Sumiyoshi Taisha is the head shrine of some 2,300 Sumiyoshi shrines across the country, and Oyosami Shrine has a history of more than 2,000 years.
