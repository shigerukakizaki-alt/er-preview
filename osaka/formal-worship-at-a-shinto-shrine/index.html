What happens at a formal worship at a Shinto shrine

Most visitors pray in front of the hall. A formal worship takes you inside it. Here is what to expect, step by step.

At most shrines in Japan, visitors stand before the hall, bow, clap and leave. A formal worship, seishiki sanpai, is different: you are invited inside the hall, and a priest performs a ceremony for you. It is how Japanese families mark a new business, an anniversary or a milestone birthday.

1. Purify your hands

Every visit begins at the water basin. You rinse your left hand, then your right, and your mouth, with a wooden ladle. It is the first act of respect at any shrine.

2. Change into ceremonial dress

For our experiences you change into formal dress, with professional dressers to help. It changes how the day feels: you are no longer a spectator.

3. The purification and the prayer

Inside the hall, the priest waves a wand of white paper streamers over you to purify you, then recites a prayer on your behalf. You bow twice, clap twice and bow once more. The priest shows you when.

4. A dance for the gods

At Sumiyoshi Taisha, a shrine maiden performs a kagura dance in the Kaguraden for your party alone. At Oyosami Shrine, the whole precinct is closed to others for your visit, and tea ceremony follows in the same quiet hall.

You do not need to be religious. Shrines welcome everyone, and the priest guides you through each step. Photographs are welcome, except during the blessing itself.
