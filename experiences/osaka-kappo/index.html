Osaka Kappo: A Michelin-Starred Counter, Explained

The chef's course at a Michelin-starred counter, explained dish by dish by an Osaka food writer.

THE EXPERIENCE

Kappo means "to cut and to cook". It is Japan's counter cuisine, where the chef prepares each course in front of you, and the counter style is said to have begun in Osaka. With one of Osaka's food writers as your host, you enjoy the chef's course at a Michelin-starred kappo restaurant, explained dish by dish.

Your host has written about Osaka's restaurants for many years, in magazines and in books. Between courses you hear what the chef is doing and why: the season behind each ingredient, the knife work, the tableware and the story of the restaurant. Conversation with the chef across the counter is interpreted, so you can ask anything.

How the day unfolds

  1. 1

    Meet your host at the restaurant

    A short introduction to kappo, and to the restaurant you are about to visit.

  2. 2

    Take your seats at the counter

    The counter is the stage. You watch the chef prepare each course a few feet away.

  3. 3

    Seasonal small dishes

    The first courses show the season on the plate. Your host explains the ingredients and how they are cut and arranged.

  4. 4

    The soup bowl

    A clear soup in a lacquered bowl, often called the heart of a Japanese meal. Lift the lid and take in the aroma first.

  5. 5

    Sake chosen for each course

    Sake and other drinks are paired with the courses, and your host tells you about each one.

  6. 6

    Talk with the chef

    Ask about the ingredients, the knives or the tableware. Your host interprets every word.
